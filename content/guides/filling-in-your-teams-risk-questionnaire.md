The Risk site asks each team a set of yes/no and number questions about how it works. From the answers, the site rates the risks that apply to your team and shows where to focus. The goal is simple: raise the risks, so the right treatment can be agreed. This guide is for team leads and the people on a team who answer for it.

## Who your team is

On the Risk site, a group is one of TRUST's teams. That is a product family, or each L3's organization, depending on how TRUST groups its teams. Your team's lead, its people and their count come from TRUST, so you do not set them up on the Risk site.

The front page lists the teams you can open. Each shows its status:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| Not started | Nobody has answered yet. |
| Draft | Answers are saved, but nothing has been submitted, or answers changed after the last submit. |
| Submitted | The latest answers have been submitted and rated. |
| Signed off | The L3 (or their delegate, or the approver) has signed the assessment off. |
| Sent back | The assessment went back to the team with a reason. The team can answer and submit again. |

If you see no teams, TRUST may not hold teams yet, or you may not be one of a team's people. Tech Risk can help.

## Who can answer and submit

- **See the questionnaire:** the team's people, the managers above them, Tech Risk, auditors, and leadership.
- **Answer:** the team's people (the lead and everyone under them), the managers above them, and Tech Risk. An answer saved by Tech Risk is marked "from Tech Risk".
- **Submit:** the team's lead, the managers above the team, and Tech Risk.

If you can look but not answer, the page says so at the top. Letting a delegate submit for a lead is Wanted.

## Answer one section at a time

Open your team and choose "Start the questionnaire" (or "Continue the questionnaire"). The form shows one section at a time, with Back and Next and a line such as "Section 2 of 4". The site remembers which section you were on.

Each question has a driver code, such as RD01. A driver is something about how your team works that can raise a risk. **Yes always means the sentence is true for your team.** Questions with a number ask you to type it. For a percent, type the % sign, for example 25%.

**Every answer is saved the moment you change it.** The line under the question says who saved it and when, so you can stop and come back. You can also clear an answer, and the line then says it was cleared.

A question with no answer shows as "not answered yet". The site never guesses it and never counts it as on.

## Questions answered from data

Some drivers will be answered from data instead of from this form. That is Wanted: nothing is pulled from data yet. On the last section, a closed box lists these drivers, so you can see which ones are not on the form. They show as "not answered yet" in the results.

## Submit

On the last section, choose Submit. If some questions have no answer, the site lists them first, each with a link to its section. You can choose "Keep answering", or "Submit anyway" if it is fine to leave them.

When you submit, the site rates every risk for your team from the saved answers and keeps the result as an assessment. You land on the results page.

## What happens next

- Your team's results are on its own page. [Reading your results and the risk heat map](/guides/reading-your-results-and-the-risk-heat-map/) explains them.
- The L3 over your lead signs the assessment off, or sends it back with a note. See [Signing off a risk assessment](/guides/signing-off-a-risk-assessment/). A sent-back assessment shows its reason on the front page, on the questionnaire and on the results page.
- If you change an answer after submitting, the team goes back to Draft. Submit again to make a new assessment. Earlier assessments are kept, so you can see how things change.

> **Example.** A team answers 20 questions in two sittings. It leaves 2 blank and chooses Submit. The site lists those 2 questions. The lead chooses "Submit anyway". The results show the 2 drivers as "not answered yet".

If you're unsure, Tech Risk can help.
