Once a team submits its questionnaire, the Risk site rates each risk and shows the results. The goal is to raise the risks, so the right treatment can be agreed. This guide is for team leads, their people and leadership. It covers your team's own results and the firm-wide risk heat map.

Each team's results are on that team's own page. They are not compared with other teams. The only firm-wide view is the heat map, and it shows totals only.

## Likelihood and impact

Each risk gets a likelihood and an impact. Each is one of four levels: Low, Medium, High and Critical. The site shows the level as a word and four small dots, one dot for Low up to four for Critical.

- **Likelihood** is how likely the risk is for your team, from the drivers that are on.
- **Impact** is how bad it would be. The site rates three kinds of impact, and the overall impact is the highest of the three.
- A risk **does not apply** when none of its own drivers is on. These are listed last, under "Does not apply to this group".

## Elevated

A risk is **Elevated** when both its likelihood and its impact are High or Critical. Tech Risk can change this level in the rating settings. The top of the page counts the Elevated risks, out of the risks that apply, and "Where to focus" lists them. If nothing is Elevated, that box is left out.

Elevated risks are the ones to talk about first. They are not a verdict on the team. A risk that applies but has no impact rating is never Elevated.

## How this was rated

Every risk has a closed "How this was rated" box. It shows each step in words: which drivers are on, how the likelihood was reached, and how each kind of impact was reached, with your team's own numbers. Each risk also lists the drivers that are on, with their driver codes, and a tag for its building block.

Drivers with no answer are never counted as on. The page says how many drivers are "not answered yet".

## Trends against your previous assessment

From the second assessment on, each risk shows how it moved since your team's own previous assessment:

- ▲ Better
- ▼ Worse
- ▬ No change
- Mixed, when one rating went up and the other went down

The first assessment has no trend yet. A drop-down above the list lets you open an earlier assessment.

## Controls from TRAC

Under a risk, "Controls that address this risk" lists the TRAC controls that cover it, each linking to TRAC. The list's date shows small, for example "Controls as of 15 Nov 2026". Which controls address each risk is Wanted for any risk TRAC has not mapped yet. If no risk has a control, the page says so in one line.

## Your team on TRUST

On TRUST, a team's own page shows its Elevated risks in a section called "Risks we have raised". It appears when all the team's people sit in one product family. It shows only that team's results, not anyone else's.

## The risk heat map

Open "Risk heat map" in the top bar. Everyone who can open the Risk site can see it, because it holds totals only. It adds up the newest assessment of every team that has one. It never shows which team a risk came from.

- **The headline** is how many risks are Elevated, out of how many assessed risks, and how many teams have assessed (a count only).
- **The grid** is four by four. Likelihood runs down, with Critical at the top, and impact runs across. Each box counts the risks in it. Shaded boxes are Elevated. Choose a box to list its risks, each with a plain description, an example and the controls TRAC lists for it.
- **Where to focus** lists the Elevated risks, most serious first, and how many assessments have each one Elevated.
- **Since previous assessments** gives totals, such as "2 risks better, 1 worse". Each assessment is compared only with its own earlier one.
- **Building block** narrows every total to one building block.

The heat map shows Elevated as it was decided when each team submitted, so it agrees with each team's results page. A settings change shows from the next submit.

## Choosing a treatment

Raising a risk is the first step. The next step is agreeing how to respond to it. [Choosing how to respond to a risk](/guides/choosing-how-to-respond-to-a-risk/) walks through the four ways to respond, and why it helps to write down the one you chose. The heat map and the "Where to focus" box link to it.

> **Example.** A team opens its results and sees 3 Elevated risks out of 9 that apply. It starts with the first one, opens "How this was rated" to see which drivers are on, checks the TRAC controls listed under it, and agrees a treatment.
